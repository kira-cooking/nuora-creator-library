the shape of it

Where every second went

0:00 – 0:05

She shows a belly before she says one word you can follow

Play the first second again. Tito hasn’t said a word that means much yet, and there is already a photo up in the corner of a woman pulling her shirt over her lower belly.

Every woman who sees that photo looks down at her own stomach. It’s automatic. The scroll stopped right there, before the words even landed.

“I’ll say this a million times. This is high cortisol. This is high cortisol. This is high cortisol. And this is high cortisol.”

Now listen to how she says it. The words are "this is," never "you are." Her finger is pointed at a stranger in a photo, so the woman watching gets to think "oh, that’s me" in her own head, and nobody made her admit it out loud.

Four photos, four times, in five seconds. Only one of them has to be your problem for the video to have you.

Every move after that, in order

0:050:09

She talks about her clients, so nobody puts a guard up

“Those are some reasons I recommend this magnesium complex to all my clients.”

She never says "you should buy this." One word does all of it, and the word is clients. It moves who the sentence is for. The woman watching feels like she’s listening in on advice meant for somebody else, and people don’t argue with advice they’re overhearing.

So talk about what you tell your sister, your friends, the women who message you asking. Let her listen in.

0:090:13

She answers "who are you" before anyone gets to ask it

By now there’s one question sitting in the viewer’s head: "okay, but who are you?"

“I’ve been a nutrition coach in fitness training for the last 27 years, plus I turned 50 this year.”

Three answers land at once. First the 27 years and the 50th birthday. Then her certificate goes up on screen with her name on it. And underneath both of those, she’s been standing there in a sports bra and shorts the whole time, at 50.

The certificate is the receipt. Her body is the proof, and she never once asks you to look at it, because the camera already did that in the first frame, while her mouth was busy on her client list.

The timing matters as much as the answer. She got there before the viewer did, so no gap ever opened for doubt to sit in.

Answer "who are you" by the ten second mark, before she has to wonder.
