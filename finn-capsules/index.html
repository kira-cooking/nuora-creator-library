02 / WHY EACH SCENE IS THERE

Six beats.
One connected thought.

Read the scene, then the lesson. Tap a timestamp to jump back to that moment in the video.

01 / RECOGNITION

The subject is clear before she finishes speaking.

A woman checks a tissue in the bathroom. The full opening sentence, “The first day you’ll discharge 10 times,” is already on screen. The action and caption establish the concern immediately.

Take this into your video

Show the moment as you name it. If your line is about feeling uncomfortable at dinner, begin at the table. Make the first frame understandable with the sound off.

02 / AN ANSWER

It does not leave the opening hanging.

The next scene moves into an anatomical illustration. The narrator offers a reason for what the viewer just saw. The picture follows the sentence, so the story keeps moving.

Take this into your video

Pay off the question you open. If you say “Turn the bottle around,” actually show the label and explain what to check. A diagram can illustrate a claim; it cannot prove it.

03 / A HUMAN STAKE

“Confidence” becomes something you can picture.

The ad talks about second showers and awkward bathroom checks. These ordinary actions make an abstract feeling specific. They give the viewer something to recognize.

Take this into your video

For capsules, use one real moment: adjusting your clothes during dinner, choosing an untucked shirt, or thinking about your stomach on an evening out. Do not borrow the ad’s day-five or day-ten promises.

04 / STAY WITH IT

One concern carries the middle.

The story returns to checking before intimacy. It continues developing the same concern instead of introducing a new reason to buy every few seconds.

Take this into your video

If you opened with comfort after meals, stay with that. Adding weight, energy, hormones and sleep gives the viewer four more stories to follow.

05 / MAKE IT TANGIBLE

Now there is a product to attach the story to.

The pack first appears around 51 seconds. Later, the character holds and eats a gummy. The name, physical product and action finally connect.

Take this into your video

Show the real capsule bottle and the capsule itself. Explain what you can actually demonstrate. Our shorter test introduces the product earlier; Finn’s timing is an observation, not a rule to copy.

06 / A NEXT STEP

The ending leaves one clear action.

The story returns to confidence, compares alternatives, and ends with a clean pack shot and “Grab Nuora now.” The viewer knows where the ad is asking them to go.

Take this into your video

End with one instruction that matches the landing page: see the ingredients, directions and current options. Leave enough time to read it.

THE PATTERN TO BORROW

A recognizable moment → A question → A useful answer → The product → A clear next step

What the results tell us, and what they do not

Finn’s ad recorded 329 attributed purchases and 1.41 revenue ROAS from October 21 to November 5, 2026, using Meta’s seven-day-click attribution. November 5 alone reached 2.16 ROAS; that was not its sustained result.

These are results for the whole ad and its delivery context, not proof that any one line caused purchases. The capsule ideas below are proposed tests. All three reviewed Finn variants introduced the pack around 49–51 seconds, so late product timing does not explain why this version received most of the delivery.

She may want to enjoy dinner.
Start there.

Nuora Gut Ritual is the capsule product for this brief. Finn’s example is for intimate-wellness gummies. Keep the customer concern and product story separate.

A meal where she is thinking about her stomach instead of the conversation.

THE WARDROBE

“I want to feel comfortable in this.”

Choosing between a tucked and untucked shirt. Clothes she already owns.

“What am I actually choosing?”

Comparing another supplement after trying things before. Wanting clear details.

Illustrative moments informed by historical customer feedback, not customer testimonials or promised product results.

Three ways in.
The same story after.

Film all three openings, then attach each to the shared body below. Keep the body, length and call to action the same so the opening is the main thing that changes.

Use personal lines only when they describe your real experience. Otherwise use the observer version shown with each hook. These are new directions to test, not proven winners.

somewhere to go.

This is a selection story: help her understand what to check before choosing a supplement. It does not require you to invent a personal result. Aim for 40–55 seconds total, including your opening.

“It’s a capsule, so there’s no powder to mix into a drink. Turn the bottle around: check the ingredients and serving directions before adding it to your routine.”

Turn to the current label. Point to ingredients, then directions as you mention them. Keep the text readable.

0434–44sMake it useful
“Then check the order itself: how much you’re getting, and whether the option you choose refills.”

Show the current purchase options for your audience’s country. Clearly indicate the option you are describing.

“You can see the ingredients, directions and current options through the link.”

Finish on the real bottle. Overlay: “See the product details.” Leave it on screen long enough to read.

Use your natural speaking rhythm. Timings are a guide. Check the bottle and current product page before recording specific ingredients, serving amounts, prices or terms.

05 / WHAT TO FILM & SEND

One filming session.
Three complete versions.

Your footage checklist

  • Three separate opening takes, each with its matching scene.
  • One clean take of the shared story body.
  • Mirror / shirt cutaway and dinner-table cutaway.
  • Bottle front, capsule close-up and readable back label.
  • Current purchase-options screen and a clean final pack shot.

Your finished edits

  • Three vertical 9:16 videos: A · Outfit, B · Dinner, C · Decision.
  • The same body, captions and ending after each opening.
  • Clear voice, natural light and captions away from app controls.
  • No invented personal results, medical animation as proof, or borrowed “day X” promises.
AFTER THE OPENING TEST

Then change the way you tell the body.

Keep the strongest opening and compare two versions: you speaking with cutaways versus a hands-and-product demonstration with the same narration. Keep the words and ending consistent. This tests the scene treatment without changing the entire story at once.

Review which opening holds attention and leads to useful clicks and purchases. More views alone do not tell us whether the story worked.
