the big idea

Yours needs a movie, not a symptom

The ad does not open on anyone’s pain. It opens on a story big enough that you have to hear the end, and then it goes deep. Look at what is actually in it:

  1. A worldA mansion in the Hamptons, and a family where nobody ever seems to get old.
  2. A mysteryThey eat red meat and dessert, never exercise, and nobody gets sick.
  3. StakesHis own dad is dying, and he has already watched his grandfather go at 57.
  4. A way inSomeone inside the house finally tells him the secret.
  5. A riskHe takes three jars from the cellar late at night.
  6. A costThey get caught and thrown out.
  7. A happy endingHis dad is 85 and still walking his own dog.

Seven pieces, and the product is not one of them. It only turns up once the movie is over.

And it never goes in a straight line

Look at how the feeling moves. Every drop makes the next high feel bigger, and that is what keeps people watching for seven minutes.

yours has to be true

You are a real person, and that is your whole advantage over a cartoon. But almost every true story has a movie inside it. Start at the most dramatic moment instead of at the beginning, and go deep into the details: who was there, what was said, what it cost you, what almost went wrong.

the part that makes it sell

How to connect your story to the product

Your story does not have to be about the problem at all. We have seen UGC ads that were just a woman telling a story about her mother, and the product only came into it at the very end. People stay because they want to know how the story ends, and by the end they trust you as a person. The product gets all of that trust.

So the story itself is the easy part. The hard part is the moment it turns into the product. If it is done badly, it feels like a trick. When it is done well, the product shows up and she thinks "of course", because it feels like the only way your story could have ended.

the one mistake that breaks it

A great story, and then "anyway, here are my gummies." She feels like she was tricked into watching an ad, and a woman who feels tricked does not buy. So the story and the product have to be joined up properly, and below are four ways to do it.

Four ways to join them

You only need one. The best videos use two or three together.

bridge 1

Plant it early

what it does
Mention a small detail in the first half of your story that you do not explain yet. Then pay it off at the end.
why it works
The ad does exactly this. At 1:17 he says the family took one small habit every morning, and you only find out at 6:22 what it was. The whole time in between, part of her brain is waiting for that answer, so when it finally comes it feels like a reward instead of a pitch.
making it yours
You could mention the one habit you never skipped on a trip, or a change your friend noticed before you said a word, and only explain it at the end.
bridge 2

Your worst moment becomes the turning point

what it does
The most dramatic moment in your story becomes the day you decided to stop putting up with it.
why it works
The drama stops being random and becomes the reason you went looking for an answer. So the product arrives as the end of your search, and she has been on that search with you the whole time.
making it yours
The wedding where you would not dance. The trip you spent half of worrying. The fight with your mom that was really about what you had been hiding. For the gummies this one is often the strongest, because it is a very private problem that shows up in very public moments.
bridge 3

The same truth, in a different place

what it does
Your story teaches a truth that turns out to be exactly how the product works.
why it works
She understands the mechanism before you have even said the word. Picture a wall in your mom’s house that got repainted every spring because the damp kept coming back, until somebody found the leak behind it. Painting over it never worked, and fixing the leak did. That is the biofilm, in a different house.
making it yours
Only use a story like this if it really happened to you. Weeds that kept coming back because the roots stayed, a stain that kept showing through the paint. Then say, in your own words, that the same was happening in your body.
bridge 4

Back to the first scene

what it does
End exactly where you started, and show what is different now.
why it works
The ad’s last line goes straight back to the cellar from the very start, so the whole video feels finished. Your ending does the same for her, and a finished story feels true.
making it yours
Back to your mom, the trip, the wedding, and what is different there now for you. Then the link, once.
the test before you post

Watch your video back and pause it the second the product shows up. Then ask yourself one question: would she think "of course", or "wait, what"? If it is the second one, the story and the product are not joined up yet. You could plant a detail earlier, or pick a story whose worst moment sits closer to what the product helps with.

One story, all the way through

Here is one example of how a story could be structured, from the first second all the way to the product. Use the shape with your own real story, because these are beats and the words stay yours. Under each beat is the matching line from the ad, so you can hear how it sounded when it worked. It fits the gummies or the capsules, and only the hidden problem and the pairing change.

  1. The sceneA family dinner that ends with you and someone you love not speaking.“I worked 17 years as their gardener.”
  2. The dropWhat the argument was really about. You had been cancelling on her for months and never told her why.“But my father was dying.”
  3. The reasonWhat you had been hiding, a problem you were too embarrassed to say out loud, even to her.“He was 68, high blood pressure for over a decade, blood sugar climbing every year.”
  4. The turning pointThat night was when you decided to stop hiding and find an answer.“And I wasn’t gonna watch it happen.”
  5. The mysteryEverything you had already tried, and why none of it lasted.“It made no sense to me.”
  6. The secret comes outShe finally tells you what she has never said out loud. The same struggle ran through the women in your family, and there was a way they dealt with it that nobody ever talked about.“Promise me you’ll never tell anyone.”
  7. What the secret actually isTwo ingredients that only work together. On its own either one does very little, and that is why everything you bought before did nothing. For the capsules that is berberine with black pepper. For the gummies it is the enzyme that clears the way for the probiotic.“Not the kind you cook. They seal it in jars and leave it in the dark.”
  8. The highThe next time you saw her, and what was different for you.“By the end of the second month he wasn’t falling asleep at seven anymore.”
  9. Back to the first sceneThe same table, a year later. Then the product, once.“You’ll understand why a family worth billions keeps a cellar full of garlic under their house.”

Look at where the product sits. It is the very last beat, and by then it feels like the only way that story could end. Now look at the ups and downs: a fight, a secret, a decision, a search, an answer, a happy ending. That is the same rollercoaster as the ad, with none of the drama made up.

For the capsules, the ingredients to talk about are bromelain, berberine, black pepper, ashwagandha and dandelion.

your version

The same idea, in your own format

Longer is fine when the story is good. Two or three minutes works, because people stay for a story. The only rule is that the story comes first and the product comes last.

Five beats, for a two minute video

The direction is ours. The words and the story are yours.

0:00–0:15

Open on a scene, not a problem

what it does
Start in the middle of a moment that makes her need to know what happens next.
why it works
The ad opens on a secret, never a symptom, so nobody feels like they are watching an ad. A scene pulls her in before her guard goes up.
making it yours
A fight with your mom, a trip that went wrong, the night your friend who is a nurse told you what she sees at work, or the moment it all started for you. Pick the real one.
0:15–0:45

The mystery

what it does
Somebody had what you wanted, or a detail did not add up, and you did not know why.
why it works
That family ate dessert every night and never got sick, so you had to find out. A question with no answer yet is what keeps people watching.
making it yours
A woman who never seemed to have the problem you had, a question your mom never answered, or everything that should have worked and did not. Any real question you truly wanted answered works.
0:45–1:15

How the secret reached you, told like a scene

what it does
This is where your story turns toward the product, so use one of the four bridges above. Go deep here: where you were, who told you, what they said, how you felt hearing it.
why it works
This is the part of the ad people remember, the night the secret came out. The more real detail you give, the more it feels like a film and the less it feels like a pitch.
making it yours
Then explain it simply. Bad bacteria build a biofilm, like plaque on teeth, so most of what she took never got through it. Bromelain, the enzyme from pineapple stems, helps break it down. Or keep it even simpler and talk about the pairing, two ingredients that only work together.
1:15–1:45

Show it working, and what it cost you

what it does
Small real changes in the order they happened, starting with the slow weeks. Plus whatever it really cost you, or what almost went wrong.
why it works
His dad felt nothing for two weeks, and saying so made every part after it believable. The cost is a drop on the rollercoaster, and it is what turns a testimonial into a story.
making it yours
Keep it to what you actually saw, for you or for her. Never what will happen to the person watching.
1:45–end

The test, then the product

what it does
Show her how to check her own bottle, then bring out yours.
why it works
His test was yellow against dark amber. Ours is even simpler. Pineapple flavouring on a label is different from bromelain, the enzyme from the stem. She can look at what she already owns and find out for herself.
making it yours
Then the pouch or the capsules, where the link is, and stop. The quieter the close, the more she trusts it.
the one line to take
Seven minutes long, and what everyone remembers is a man still walking his dog at 85.
